How Often Should You Mow Your Lawn? A Redlands Season-by-Season Guide

In the Redlands, most lawns need mowing every 7–14 days from spring to autumn and every 3–4 weeks in winter. Here's how to set the right schedule for your grass.

Short answer: in the Redlands and greater Brisbane, most lawns need mowing every 7 to 14 days from September to April, and every 3 to 4 weeks from May to August. The exact frequency depends on your grass type, how much rain we've had, and how short you like it.

South-East Queensland's warm, wet summers mean warm-season grasses like couch, buffalo, zoysia and kikuyu grow fast for most of the year. Mow too rarely and you end up cutting off too much at once, which stresses the lawn. Mow too often in winter and you wear it out for no benefit.

The one-third rule

The most useful mowing rule is simple: never remove more than a third of the leaf height in one cut. If you keep your buffalo at 50 mm, mow it before it reaches about 75 mm. Cutting off more than that shocks the grass, exposes the brown stems underneath, and can scalp the lawn. That's the main reason an overgrown lawn looks patchy for weeks after a hard cut.

How often to mow, month by month

SeasonTypical Redlands growthHow often to mow
Spring (Sep–Nov)Growth picks up quickly as it warmsEvery 10–14 days
Summer (Dec–Feb)Peak growth, especially after rainEvery 7–10 days
Autumn (Mar–May)Still growing, slowing by MayEvery 10–14 days
Winter (Jun–Aug)Slow; couch may go semi-dormantEvery 3–4 weeks

A wet summer can push lawns to need mowing every week; a dry spell can stretch it to a fortnight or more. Watch the grass, not just the calendar.

Mowing height by grass type

As a rule of thumb for South-East Queensland lawns (raise the height a notch in summer heat and in shady areas):

  • Buffalo (e.g. Sir Walter): around 40–60 mm. Buffalo hates being scalped.
  • Couch: around 15–30 mm. Couch tolerates a low cut, but not in the middle of a heatwave.
  • Zoysia: around 20–40 mm.
  • Kikuyu: around 30–50 mm. It grows very fast in summer, so it often needs weekly mowing.

Taller grass shades its own soil, holds moisture better through a Queensland summer, and crowds out weeds. Most lawn problems we see in the Redlands come from mowing too short, not too long.

Signs your mowing schedule isn't right

  • Brown, stalky patches after mowing: you're cutting too much at once or too low. Mow more often, or raise the height. (See what causes brown patches.)
  • Seed heads everywhere: the lawn is getting away between mows.
  • Clumps of clippings left on top: the grass was too long or wet when cut.
  • Weeds taking over thin areas: the lawn is too short or too sparse to compete. Pair a better schedule with weed spraying.

Don't forget the hours

In Queensland you can mow from 7am to 7pm Monday to Saturday and 8am to 7pm on Sundays and public holidays. More in our guide to what time you can mow in QLD.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I mow my lawn in summer in Brisbane and the Redlands?

Every 7 to 10 days for most lawns, and weekly for fast growers like kikuyu after rain.

How often should I mow in winter?

Every three to four weeks is usually enough. Couch lawns may barely grow in the coolest months.

Is it bad to mow too often?

Mowing more often isn't harmful if you only take the top third off, but in winter it adds wear without much benefit. Cutting too short is the bigger risk.

What height should I mow buffalo grass?

Around 40–60 mm as a rule of thumb, a little higher in summer and in shade.
